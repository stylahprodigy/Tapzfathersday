The Journal of Him

Every year, I strive to create something truly unforgettable when giving gifts — and this is my 2026 Father’s Day tribute. Kept live online for the entire year so our family can forever revisit these memories, this archive is a window into the life of my father: a man whose easy humor, calm mannerisms, and gentle wisdom taught us to always smile and laugh things off, knowing that no storm lasts forever.

ENTRY I • FAITH & INTEGRITY

A Man Who
Showed Up,

People knew my dad for many things — builder, mechanic, comedian, gardener. But above all, he was first and foremost a man who showed up: a father who was always there whenever we needed him most. This journal is a token of my deepest appreciation for the man he has always been.

🕊️

A Foundation of Faith & Example

Thank you, Dad, for always keeping us accountable, guiding us to church, and helping build a foundation of morals and faith that shape who we are today.

Thank you for always leading by example — staying behind to clean up at church functions, standing as a true pioneer in our community, and watching over us through every spiritual battle.

ENTRY II • THE PILLAR & GUIDE

The Hands That Held Us

Dear Dad,

Thank you for being our constant anchor, our loudest supporter, and the greatest example of what it means to lead with love, integrity, and quiet strength.

Every sacrifice you made, every lesson you taught, and every laugh we shared has shaped who we are today.

ENTRY III • THE TIMELINE

a reel of the years

2005

Island Warmth & Kinship

Captured in genuine company with his sister-in-law — a true testament to his natural warmth. Dad has that rare gift of getting along with everyone he meets, turning strangers into lifelong friends anywhere he goes.

2007

Shared Laughs & Grace

Every memory of Dad is filled with laughter. He was always the first to smile, finding joy in the simplest moments and lifting every room with his contagious, easygoing laughter.

2009

The Glow Between Them

Two people who had learned, over years, to live in each other's light. The flare in the lens was only the sun doing what their faces had already done — refusing to let us tell where one ended and the other began.

2014

What He Grew

Not crops, not wealth — people. A girl who learned to run in his shadow, a child who learned to sit on his shoulders. The tattoo told of where he'd been; the children told of where he stayed.
